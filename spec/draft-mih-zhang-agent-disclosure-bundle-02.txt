                          AAC Evidence Bundle
               draft-mih-zhang-agent-disclosure-bundle-02

Abstract

   This document defines the AAC Evidence Bundle, a portable
   presentation and verification container for an Agent Action Capsule
   and the records that make its evidentiary claim intelligible.  A
   permalink carries a bundle in its URL fragment, an offline HTML
   report carries a bundle in its shell, and a hosted report serves a
   bundle at its URL.  The bundle does not alter any enclosed Capsule.
   It declares its citation closure and any missing cited records,
   carries verified disclosure preimages as a bundle-level overlay,
   separates three different completeness claims, can carry the
   producer's signed declaration of what it offers in each category of
   evidence, and permits independently specified extension blocks and
   countersignatures: signatures by a party other than the producer over
   the bundle digest, each accompanied by a statement of which claims
   that party recomputed and with what result.

1.  Introduction

   A Capsule proves the identity and claimed content of one action.  An
   audit report normally needs more: the report Capsule, its chain of
   judgments or adjudications, the turns cited by those records,
   selected disclosed preimages, and the log evidence that supports a
   claim about coverage.  Passing these items as unrelated files
   produces a presentation that a verifier cannot describe precisely.

   The AAC Evidence Bundle is that unit of evidence.  It is deliberately
   a presentation-layer object: the Capsules in records retain their
   original bytes, identities, signatures, and registrations.  The same
   bundle can be encoded in a URL fragment, embedded in an offline HTML
   shell, or obtained from a hosted report URL.  These are transports
   for one object, not distinct evidence formats.

2.  Conventions and Definitions

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
   "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP
   14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
   capitals, as shown here.

   Bundle:  An AAC Evidence Bundle as defined in Section 3.

   Root Capsule:  The Capsule identified by the Bundle's root member.
      The Bundle's evidence claim is made from this Capsule outward
      through its declared closure.

   JSON-DIGEST:  The lowercase-hex SHA-256 digest of UTF8(JCS(value)),
      using [RFC8785] over the whole recursively canonicalized JSON
      value with no member filtering, as defined by
      [I-D.mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule].

   Countersignature:  A countersignature is a signature by a party other
      than the producer over a bundle digest, accompanied by a statement
      of which claims that party recomputed and with what result.  A
      report is a bundle.

   Countersigner:  The party that makes a countersignature.  A



Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                   [Page 3]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


      countersigner works in the manner of an Auditor [RFC9943]: it
      independently recomputes checks over a Bundle it was given and
      signs a statement of the results -- a list of what it recomputed,
      never an opinion.  A countersigner MAY register that statement as
      a Signed Statement with a Transparency Service, in which case the
      entry carries the Receipt; the Receipt proves that the statement
      is in that log and says nothing about the Bundle.  Which producers
      a countersigner accepts Bundles from is its own admission rule,
      outside this document.

   Self-countersignature:  An entry in countersignatures whose signer
      key is the producer's key (Section 9.3).  It is well-formed and is
      rendered as not independent.

   countersignatures is a member name.  An entry is a statement by a
   party other than the producer that it recomputed named checks over
   the bundle digest, in the manner of an Auditor [RFC9943].  It is not
   a COSE countersignature [RFC9338], not a Receipt [RFC9942], and not a
   Transparency Service function.

   WITHHELD:  The disclosure state of an eligible member absent from the
      Bundle-level disclosure overlay.  It is neither an empty value nor
      a verification failure.

   The terms "Capsule", "capsule_id", chain.parent_capsule_id,
   references, seq, leaf_index, and log_coordinates are as defined in
   [I-D.mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule].

   {
     "bundle_version": "2",
     "bundle_kind": "evidence-bundle/v2",
     "root": "<capsule_id>",
     "records": [ ... ],
     "completeness": {
       "closure_depth": 2,
       "records_mode": "complete|declared_incomplete",
       "payloads_mode": "all|selected|none",
       "suppressed_fields": [ ... ],
       "missing": [ "<cited capsule digest>", ... ],
       "selection": "producer-selected"
     },
     "disclosures": {
       "<capsule_id>": { "<member>": "<revealed preimage>" }
     },
     "disclosure_record": "<capsule_id>",
     "completeness_certificate": { ... },
     "checkpoint": { ... },
     "category_coverage": { ... },
     "verification": { ... },
     "extensions": { "<registered kind>": { ... } },
     "countersignatures": [ ... ]
   }

   bundle_version and bundle_kind are REQUIRED and MUST be respectively
   "2" and "evidence-bundle/v2". root, records, and completeness are
   REQUIRED, except that an empty-history Bundle (Section 3.1) has no
   root. root is the lowercase-hex capsule_id of one record in records.
   Every records element MUST be an unmodified Capsule object conforming
   to [I-D.mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule]; duplicate capsule_id values
   are not permitted.  All other members shown are OPTIONAL unless a
   claim in this document requires them.  A receiver MUST reject an
   object with an unknown bundle_version or bundle_kind as an AAC
   Evidence Bundle, rather than silently applying version-2 verification
   rules.

   payloads_mode states whether the producer included all payload
   material it chose to carry (all), only a selected subset (selected),
   or none (none).  A Bundle whose payloads_mode is none carries no
   disclosures member. selection, when present, names the certificate
   mode of completeness_certificate (Section 6.1). suppressed_fields
   names fields intentionally not carried by the Bundle; it does not
   turn an absent disclosure into an empty value or a failure.

3.1.  Empty-History Bundles

   An _empty-history Bundle_ shows that a log stream has no history to
   disclose.  A stream that was never started and a history that was
   cleared read the same.  Clearing a history starts a new stream, with
   a new log_id, under the same key.  It never rewrites or truncates the
   old stream's log, and a checkpoint that does so is inconsistent with
   that log's earlier checkpoints.

   An empty-history Bundle has an empty records array and no root
   member. root MUST be absent exactly when records is empty.  In an
   empty-history Bundle:

   *  completeness.records_mode MUST be complete, completeness.missing
      MUST be absent or empty, and completeness.payloads_mode MUST be
      none;

   *  completeness_certificate, disclosures and disclosure_record MUST
      be absent;

   *  checkpoint, with checkpoint.cose, SHOULD be present when the
      stream has a checkpoint: its first checkpoint, or its latest one
      while it still holds no record.  A stream that was never started
      has none.

   A Bundle with no root that is not a well-formed empty-history Bundle
   is malformed.

   A verifier reports a well-formed empty-history Bundle as _no
   history_. It is well formed: a verifier MUST NOT report it as failed,
   and MUST NOT report it as an absence (Section 8.2.2).  Of its claims
   (Section 6), graph closure passes, since there is nothing to close,
   and interval coverage and per-record membership are not established,
   with the finding no_records.  When checkpoint.cose verifies, the
   verifier reports the checkpoint's signed timestamp with the result,
   as no history since that time.  Otherwise it reports no history
   without a date.

4.  Citation Closure

   Citation closure is declared, not assumed.  Starting at root, a
   producer MUST traverse chain.parent_capsule_id and every
   references[].digest transitively through completeness.closure_depth
   edges.  The default depth is 2 when the member is absent: report to
   judgments or adjudications, then to turns.  A producer using a
   different depth MUST state that non-negative JSON integer explicitly.





Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                   [Page 6]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   For each target reached within that depth, the Bundle MUST either
   supply the cited Capsule in records with a matching identity or list
   its digest once in completeness.missing.  A target MUST NOT be
   silently dropped. records_mode MUST be complete exactly when missing
   is absent or empty, and MUST be declared_incomplete exactly when it
   is non-empty.  A verifier MUST report the latter as declared
   incomplete, not as complete and not as an unexplained failure.  A
   supplied record whose computed capsule_id does not match the chain
   target or reference digest is not supplied evidence for that target.

   The closure declaration says nothing about records beyond its stated
   depth.  A verifier MUST report the depth it applied and MUST NOT
   infer unbounded ancestry or citation completeness from the presence
   of a short chain.

5.  Bundle-Level Disclosures

   disclosures is a Bundle-level overlay keyed first by the enclosed
   Capsule's capsule_id, then by the disclosure member name:

   "disclosures": {
     "<capsule_id>": {
       "agent_input": { ... },
       "agent_output": { ... }
     }
   }

   It has the same eligibility and DE-3 digest rule as the per-Capsule
   Disclosure Envelope in [I-D.mih-agent-disclosure-envelope].  The
   member name selects that draft's registered committed-digest path in
   the specified Capsule.  For every disclosed member, a verifier MUST
   compute JSON-DIGEST(revealed preimage) and compare it to the
   committed digest at that registered path in that records element.  A
   match is REVEALED; a mismatch is a failed verification of that
   disclosed content.  A member absent from this overlay is WITHHELD,
   never blank and never failed.

   The overlay is carried once per Bundle rather than by wrapping each
   Capsule in {capsule, disclosures}. It MUST NOT alter an enclosed
   Capsule or its capsule_id.  An overlay key not naming a supplied
   record, or a member not eligible under the Disclosure Envelope
   registry, is a disclosure finding and MUST NOT be treated as a
   successful revelation. disclosure_record, when present, identifies
   the Capsule that records the act of sealing or issuing these
   disclosures; it is evidence about that act and does not replace the
   DE-3 check.

6.  Completeness Claims and Log Membership

   completeness_certificate and checkpoint, when supplied, support three
   separate claims.  A verifier MUST report each claim independently.
   The composed/v1 extension adds a fourth claim, composition closure
   (Section 8.2.3), which is reported separately from these three.

   1.  *Graph closure*: every citation reached under Section 4 is
       supplied with matching identity or explicitly listed in
       completeness.missing.

   2.  *Interval coverage*: the claimed sequence interval is bound to a
       checkpointed range root.  Without a verified Receipt [RFC9942]
       over the checkpoint from a Transparency Service, or a verified
       checkpoint signature, this is only relative to a producer-
       asserted checkpoint.

   3.  *Per-record membership*: every supplied record is bound to its
       claimed log position under that range root, and, for a range,
       every other leaf in the interval is accounted for (Section 6.3).

   Per-record membership is the claim a range certificate makes about
   the records in its interval: interval coverage binds the leaf values,
   and per-record membership says what each leaf is.  A verifier MUST
   ignore members of completeness_certificate that this document does
   not define; they MUST NOT change any result it reports.

   The certificate MUST state its log_id, range root, first and last
   seq, and the proof material that binds that range root to checkpoint.
   A signed range proof that binds only the first and last record proves
   endpoint inclusion only: an interior record can be deleted or
   replaced while such a proof still passes.  A verifier MUST NOT report
   whole-range completeness from endpoint inclusion alone.  A verifier
   MUST verify an included Receipt [RFC9942] over the checkpoint, or the
   checkpoint signature, before reporting interval coverage or per-
   record membership as independently verified.  Otherwise it MUST
   explicitly label those claims checkpoint_unverified and describe them
   as relative to a producer-asserted checkpoint.  The portable
   checkpoint signature member is checkpoint.cose, an unpadded base64url
   CLL COSE checkpoint.  A verifier that implements this member MUST
   verify it and require its log identifier, MMR size, and root to equal
   the certificate and checkpoint values before removing the qualifier.

   For the third claim, every supplied record MUST either carry a
   membership entry within the declared interval or be explicitly listed
   in completeness.missing.  A membership entry or record whose seq is
   outside the interval MUST be rejected.  Each membership entry MUST
   carry log_coordinates stating its claimed position and an inclusion



Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                   [Page 8]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   proof to the certificate's range root.  The base profile's terms
   apply exactly: seq is the 1-based log position and leaf_index = seq -
   1 is the zero-based MMR index. log_coordinates states which index is
   used.  The verifier MUST verify every inclusion proof at its stated
   leaf_index, require the proof's leaf_index to equal that coordinate
   and its size to equal checkpoint.mmr_size, and bind the record to the
   range root; it MUST NOT substitute an endpoint proof for a missing
   per-record proof.  A record without a valid proof may remain usable
   for graph closure but does not satisfy per-record membership.

6.1.  Certificate Modes

   completeness.selection names the mode in which
   completeness_certificate is read:

   *  *Absent* (the Bundle has no selection member): a range
      certificate, as described above.  Its interval ends at the
      checkpoint's last leaf.

   *  *producer-selected*: a producer-selected certificate
      (Section 6.2).

   This document defines no other value.  A verifier that meets a
   selection value it does not implement, including a value that is not
   a string (null among them), MUST report interval coverage and per-
   record membership as not established (finding
   certificate_mode_unsupported).  It MUST NOT report either claim as
   failed because of the mode, MUST NOT read the certificate under
   another mode, and MUST NOT report the Bundle as invalid on that
   ground.  Graph closure, disclosures, extensions and countersignatures
   are unaffected.

   The same rule applies to a verifier that predates a mode: a verifier
   that does not implement producer-selected certificates reports such a
   Bundle's interval coverage and per-record membership as not
   established, never as failed.  A Bundle in a mode the verifier lacks
   is unverified in that respect; it is not evidence of tampering, and
   reporting it as invalid would make an honest Bundle indistinguishable
   from an altered one.  The selection member is the producer's to
   write, and so is any edit to a Bundle after it is made: setting an
   unknown mode can turn a failing claim into one that is not
   established, but never into one that is established (Section 12).

6.2.  Producer-Selected Certificates

   A producer-selected Bundle carries records that its producer chose
   from a log, each proven on its own, and makes no claim about the
   records between them.  Its completeness.selection is producer-
   selected.  Its certificate carries log_id, range_root and
   memberships.  It carries no range: first_seq, last_seq, body_digests
   and range_proof are not part of it, and a verifier does not read
   them.  It MUST NOT carry non_record_leaves (Section 6.3).

   "completeness": {
     "closure_depth": 0,
     "records_mode": "complete",
     "payloads_mode": "none",
     "suppressed_fields": [],
     "missing": [],
     "selection": "producer-selected"
   },
   "completeness_certificate": {
     "log_id": "<log identifier>",
     "range_root": "<checkpoint root>",
     "memberships": { "<capsule_id>": { "log_coordinates": { ... },
                                         "inclusion_proof": { ... } } }
   }

   A verifier that implements this mode:

   *  reports interval coverage as not established (finding
      selection_not_checkable): there is no interval;

   *  verifies per-record membership record by record. range_root MUST
      equal the checkpoint's root, and the certificate's log_id the
      checkpoint's log_id.  Each record's membership entry MUST carry
      log_coordinates naming the checkpoint's log_id, a seq of at least
      1 that no other entry claims, and leaf_index = seq - 1, and an
      inclusion proof at that index whose size equals
      checkpoint.mmr_size and that binds the record's capsule_id to the
      root.  A membership entry for a record the Bundle does not carry
      is a failure.  Otherwise the rules of Section 6 apply, including
      the checkpoint_unverified label;

   *  reports a certificate that carries non_record_leaves as invalid.









Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 10]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   A producer-selected Bundle is therefore never complete as to its log:
   a verifier MUST NOT report it as such, and its best result is that
   every carried record is a member of the checkpointed log.  A changed
   record or proof fails per-record membership exactly as in a range
   Bundle.  Graph closure, which is about citations, not log positions,
   is unaffected by the mode.

6.3.  Non-Record Leaves

   A log can hold leaves that are not records.  The Evidence Layer
   [I-D.mih-agent-evidence-layer] reserves the record_type value padding
   for records a store appends so that a checkpoint's size falls on a
   bucket boundary (its Section 12.1).  A store that pads before every
   checkpoint usually has a padding leaf at the checkpoint's last leaf,
   so a range ending there always covers one.  A range certificate
   covers such leaves in its member non_record_leaves, which carries
   each one in full:

   "non_record_leaves": [
     { "seq": 6,
       "content": { "capsule_id": "<64 hex>", "record_type": "padding",
                    "epistemic_type": "producer_claim",
                    "store_nonce": "<64 hex>" } }
   ]

   non_record_leaves is an array.  Each element is an object with seq,
   the leaf's 1-based log position within [first_seq, last_seq], and
   content, the leaf's content.  A digest alone is not accepted in place
   of content: a bare leaf value could be the identifier of a Capsule
   that the Bundle thereby hides.  Members of an element other than seq
   and content are ignored.

   *The padding leaf.* This document defines one non-record leaf type,
   padding.  The members of a padding record are those of
   [I-D.mih-agent-evidence-layer] Section 12.1: record_type padding,
   epistemic_type producer_claim, and store_nonce, 32 bytes as 64
   lowercase hexadecimal characters.  That document fixes the members
   but not their serialization or the record's identifier, so this
   document states both for a padding leaf in a Bundle.  Its content is
   a JSON object with exactly four members: those three and capsule_id.
   capsule_id is 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, and is the JSON-
   DIGEST (Section 2) of the object formed by the other three members.
   The leaf value is capsule_id.  A store's local-only producer envelope
   (signature, key_id) is not part of content.  (Moving this rule into
   the Evidence Layer awaits a later revision of that document.)

   *Checking an element.* A verifier reads the elements in order.  For
   each one:



Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 11]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   1.  An element that is not an object is malformed (finding
       non_record_leaf_element_invalid).

   2.  A seq that is not an integer within [first_seq, last_seq], that
       any membership entry states in its log_coordinates (whether or
       not that entry is otherwise valid), or that is the seq of an
       earlier element, is invalid (finding
       non_record_leaf_seq_invalid); the element accounts for no
       position.

   3.  Otherwise the element accounts for its seq.  Then:

       *  when content is not an object, or its record_type is not a
          string, the element is malformed (finding
          non_record_leaf_malformed);

       *  when record_type is not padding, the leaf is of a type this
          document does not define (finding
          non_record_leaf_type_unsupported), and the verifier checks
          nothing further about it;

       *  when the padding content does not have exactly the four
          members above, each in the form above, with epistemic_type
          equal to producer_claim, it is malformed (finding
          non_record_leaf_malformed);

       *  the verifier recomputes the leaf value from the other three
          members and requires it to equal both content.capsule_id and
          body_digests[seq - first_seq]; otherwise the leaf does not
          match (finding non_record_leaf_mismatch).

   A non_record_leaves value that is not an array is malformed (finding
   non_record_leaves_malformed) and accounts for no position.

   *Per-position accounting.* For per-record membership over a range,
   every seq in [first_seq, last_seq] MUST be accounted for by exactly
   one of a record's membership entry and a non_record_leaves element.
   Each position is then:

   *  *verified*: a record's verified membership, or a padding leaf that
      recomputes to its body digest;

   *  *unsupported*: a leaf of a type the verifier does not implement;
      or

   *  *unaccounted*: neither, or an element that failed a check above
      (finding membership_record_missing for a position with neither).




Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 12]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   Any failure fails per-record membership, whatever else is
   unsupported.  When nothing fails and some position is unsupported,
   per-record membership is not established: the verifier MUST NOT
   report it as established, and MUST NOT report it as failed.
   Otherwise the rules of Section 6 apply.  A verifier that implements
   this member therefore no longer rejects a range because it contains
   padding records, as [I-D.mih-agent-evidence-layer] Section 12.1
   requires.  A verifier that does not implement the member ignores it,
   as it ignores any member it does not know, and then reports every
   padding position as missing, which fails per-record membership.  A
   producer therefore SHOULD NOT emit non_record_leaves to readers that
   do not implement this revision; until it can, a producer-selected
   certificate states what it can prove without it.

   A non-record leaf is not a record.  It never appears in records,
   never counts as a record for graph closure, disclosures or any count
   a verifier reports, and is never the target of a citation.  Leaves
   other than padding, such as a store's checkpoint stamps, are not
   covered by this document.  A range over a log that contains one is
   reported, at best, as not established through
   non_record_leaf_type_unsupported; a producer that needs a Bundle over
   such a log without that result uses a producer-selected certificate.

7.  Category Coverage

   category_coverage, when present, is the producer's signed declaration
   of what it offers in each category of a named category set: for each
   category, exactly one status.  It answers the categories field of an
   Evidence Request ([I-D.mih-agent-evidence-request]), and a producer
   MAY include it without one. *It declares; it does not prove.* A
   verifier checks that it is well formed, who signed it, and whether it
   is consistent with what the Bundle carries.  It never establishes
   that the producer offers everything it declares, or that nothing
   exists in a category declared absent.

   "category_coverage": {
     "set": "<category set identifier>",
     "categories": {
       "<category name>": { "status": "AVAILABLE" },
       "<category name>": { "status": "UNDISCLOSED",
                            "policy_uri": "<absolute URI>" }
     },
     "request_digest": "<64 hex>",
     "signer": { "key_id": "<64 hex>" },
     "signature": "<128 hex>"
   }





Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 13]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   set is REQUIRED, a non-empty string naming a category set
   (Section 14).  A set whose identifier begins x- is private: it is not
   registered, and a verifier never treats it as known. categories is
   REQUIRED, a non-empty object keyed by category name; each value is an
   object with status and, where required, policy_uri. request_digest,
   OPTIONAL, is the digest of the Evidence Request this declaration
   answers, computed as [I-D.mih-agent-evidence-request] computes a
   request digest, in lowercase hexadecimal. signer and signature are
   OPTIONAL, and present together or not at all.  Members not defined
   here are ignored: they never make the member malformed, and a
   signature covers them.

   The member is part of the bundle digest (Section 9), so a
   countersignature binds it.

7.1.  Statuses

   Each category has exactly one status, written in upper case:

   +=============+=====================================================+
   | status      | Meaning                                             |
   +=============+=====================================================+
   | AVAILABLE   | evidence in this category is offered                |
   +-------------+-----------------------------------------------------+
   | PARTIAL     | only some evidence in this category                 |
   |             | is offered                                          |
   +-------------+-----------------------------------------------------+
   | WITHHELD    | evidence in this category may exist                 |
   |             | and is not released to this requester               |
   +-------------+-----------------------------------------------------+
   | DELETED     | evidence in this category was                       |
   |             | destroyed or has expired                            |
   +-------------+-----------------------------------------------------+
   | ABSENT      | the producer states that it holds                   |
   |             | nothing in this category                            |
   +-------------+-----------------------------------------------------+
   | UNDISCLOSED | the producer does not state which of                |
   |             | the other statuses applies, under the               |
   |             | policy policy_uri names                             |
   +-------------+-----------------------------------------------------+

                                  Table 1

   AVAILABLE, PARTIAL, WITHHELD and DELETED are retention states of the
   Evidence Layer [I-D.mih-agent-evidence-layer], with the same
   meanings, applied to a category rather than to one record's payload.
   This document defines UNDISCLOSED and ABSENT.  ABSENT is the
   producer's asserted absence in the Evidence Layer's sense ("Three



Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 14]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   Kinds of No"): its own unproven claim about its own store, never a
   non-membership proof and never the requester's recorded absence of
   [I-D.mih-agent-evidence-request].  The retention state LEGAL_HOLD is
   not a status: it changes what may happen to evidence, not what is
   offered.

   UNDISCLOSED MUST carry policy_uri, an absolute URI ([RFC3986],
   Section 4.3) naming the policy under which the status is not
   disclosed; policy_uri MAY accompany any other status.  WITHHELD and
   UNDISCLOSED are different statements and MUST NOT be conflated:
   WITHHELD says evidence may exist and is not released; UNDISCLOSED
   says nothing about whether any exists.

7.2.  Signature

   signer.key_id is a 32-byte Ed25519 [RFC8032] public key as 64
   lowercase hexadecimal characters. signature is the Ed25519 signature,
   as 128 lowercase hexadecimal characters, under that key over the
   signing input

   UTF8(JCS({"category_coverage": <the member without signature>,
             "root": <the Bundle's root>}))

   Including root binds the declaration to the root record of the Bundle
   it was made for: a signed declaration copied into a Bundle with
   another root does not verify.  It does not bind the rest of the
   Bundle; a Bundle with the same root and other records carries it
   unchanged, and only the bundle digest (Section 9) tells the two
   Bundles apart.  The signature attributes the declaration to the
   holder of the key.  It does not establish who that holder is; a
   verifier MAY compare signer.key_id with the producer's keys
   (Section 9.3) and report the result.

7.3.  Verifier Properties

   A verifier that implements this member reports the following
   properties, each as exactly one of PASS, FAIL, NOT_PRESENT,
   NOT_CHECKED and INCONCLUSIVE, as the definition of each property
   below assigns them.  A Bundle without the member has no category
   properties.

   category_coverage_form:  PASS when the member is as described above,
      including a policy_uri for every UNDISCLOSED status; otherwise
      FAIL.  When it is FAIL, every other property is NOT_CHECKED.

   category_coverage_signature:  NOT_PRESENT when the member carries no





Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 15]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


      signature; PASS when the signature verifies; FAIL otherwise,
      including when the signing input has no JCS serialization (for
      example, a number JCS cannot represent).

   category_coverage_set_registered:  NOT_PRESENT for a private (x-)
      set; INCONCLUSIVE when the verifier does not know the set; FAIL
      when it knows the set and a declared category is not one of its
      names; PASS otherwise.

   category_coverage_request:  NOT_CHECKED when the verifier does not
      have the Evidence Request; otherwise FAIL when request_digest is
      present and is not that request's digest, computed over its octets

      cannot be read, or its categories field is malformed
      ([I-D.mih-agent-evidence-request]); NOT_PRESENT when the request
      has no categories field; FAIL when the request names another set,
      or a requested category has no entry; PASS otherwise.

   category_coverage_consistency:  NOT_CHECKED when the verifier cannot

      (the set's definition says how): a record it cannot assign could

      record in a DELETED or UNDISCLOSED category is not a
      contradiction, and an AVAILABLE category with no record in the

      producer offers.

   Each property stands alone.  A verifier MUST report them separately
   and MUST NOT combine them, or the statuses, into a count, a
   percentage, a score or a claim of completeness.  It MUST NOT report a
   PASS of every property as proof that the producer offered everything
   it holds.  A FAIL of category_coverage_form,
   category_coverage_signature or category_coverage_consistency is a
   failed check of the Bundle; the other properties are reported and
   change no other result.  Category coverage is reported apart from the
   completeness claims of Section 6 and changes none of them.

   A verifier that does not implement this member follows the rule for
   any member it does not know: it reports nothing about it, and the
   member stays covered by the bundle digest.

8.  Typed Extensions

   extensions, when present, is an object keyed by a registered Evidence
   Bundle extension kind.  The member name declares the kind and its
   value is that kind's block.  The extension kind registry is
   Specification Required.  A registered specification defines its block
   shape and any semantic checks.  For example, a company-side row model
   such as report/v1 is permitted only as such an extension; this
   neutral core does not define, parse, or own its rows.

   Extensions are included in the bundle digest (Section 9).  A verifier
   that does not implement a registered extension kind MUST preserve its
   integrity status as digest-covered, report the block as
   uninterpreted, and MUST NOT apply that block's semantics.  An unknown
   kind MUST NOT alter the core Capsule, closure, disclosure, or
   membership checks.

8.1.  The producer-key/v1 Extension

   The producer-key/v1 extension kind declares the key of the producer
   that assembled the Bundle:

   "extensions": {
     "producer-key/v1": { "public_key": "<64 lowercase hex>" }
   }

   public_key is the producer's 32-byte Ed25519 [RFC8032] public key as
   64 lowercase hexadecimal characters.  The block carries one key.  A
   producer MUST NOT add other members to the block, and a verifier MUST
   ignore any it finds.  Like every extension, the block is covered by
   the bundle digest, so a countersignature's over binds the
   declaration, and adding, removing, or changing the declaration
   afterwards makes that entry invalid.

   A verifier that implements this kind uses public_key for one purpose
   only: it adds the key to the producer's keys when determining
   independence (Section 9.3), so that a countersign/v1 entry whose
   signer.key_id equals it is reported as not independent.  The
   declaration can only cause an entry to be reported as not
   independent.  It MUST NOT cause any entry to be reported as
   independent, resolved, or valid, and it MUST NOT change any other
   result.  It is not a claim of identity or of authority, and a
   verifier MUST NOT present it as one.  The absence of this extension
   says nothing about whether any entry is independent.







Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 17]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   The block is malformed when it is not an object, or when public_key
   is absent, is not a string, or is not exactly 64 lowercase
   hexadecimal characters.  A verifier MUST ignore a malformed block
   when determining independence, MUST NOT fail the Bundle or any claim
   because of it, and still treats the block as digest-covered.

8.2.  The composed/v1 Extension

   The composed/v1 extension kind carries an evidence set built from the
   answers of several responders as one portable object.  Each responder
   answered one Evidence Request ([I-D.mih-agent-evidence-request]) with
   exactly one of three outcomes:

   *  an artifact, here an Evidence Bundle;

   *  a signed refusal;

   *  a recorded absence.

   Each answer is a _member_ of the composition.  The block declares:

   *  each member's digest and outcome;

   *  the observer that produced each member;

   *  the joins between members;

   *  a _composed digest_ that a judgment or other record can cite as
      the exact evidence set it was made from.

   The containing Bundle is an ordinary evidence-bundle/v2.  Its root is
   the composing party's own record, and its core checks apply
   unchanged.  The extension adds a set of member bundles alongside that
   record; it does not change what records, root or completeness mean.

   digest-hex = text .regexp "[0-9a-f]{64}"

   A join's pointer and each compare element are JSON Pointers
   [RFC6901].  Member id values are unique, and so are observer id
   values.  Every member's observer names a declared observer.  Every
   join names two distinct declared members, in ascending order.  No two
   joins share the same members, basis and pointer. not_requested names
   participants that the composing party knew of but did not ask.  A
   participant listed there is not a member, and its identifier MUST NOT
   also appear as a member id.  A block that violates any of these rules
   is malformed.  A verifier reports a malformed block as a failed
   composed/v1 check and MUST NOT report any composition result from it.

8.2.2.  Members and Outcomes

   A member records the outcome of one request to one responder, and
   nothing else.  The three outcomes are those of
   [I-D.mih-agent-evidence-request].  They are never converted into one
   another:

   *  A refusal member exists only when the responder signed a refusal.

   *  A timeout is an absence, never a refusal.

   *  A received refusal is never recorded as an absence.

   *  A request still inside its waiting window is pending.  Pending is
      not an outcome, so a pending request MUST NOT be composed as a
      member.

   digest depends on the outcome:

   *  For artifact, it is the member Evidence Bundle's bundle digest
      (Section 9).

   *  For refusal and absence, it is the lowercase hexadecimal SHA-256
      of the UTF-8 JCS [RFC8785] serialization of the refusal or absence
      object, as carried.










Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 21]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   When the body (bundle, refusal or absence) is carried, a verifier
   MUST recompute digest from it.  For a refusal or absence, it MUST
   also require the body's request_digest to equal the member's.  A
   refusal's signature is verified under the deployment's signature
   profile.  A verifier that does not implement that profile reports the
   refusal as signature-unverified.  It does not report it as failed,
   and it does not report it as absent.

   An artifact member MAY carry response: the responder's signed
   response to the request, as received, with the artifact bytes
   removed.  A response signature that names the artifact by digest
   therefore still verifies without them.  When the bundle and response
   are both carried, a verifier MUST require the response's
   request_digest to equal the member's, and its artifact_digest to
   equal the member's digest.  Either mismatch is a failed composed/v1
   check.  This binds the artifact to the request it answered, as a
   refusal's or absence's own request_digest does.  The response's
   signature is verified under the deployment's signature profile,
   exactly as a refusal's is.  A verifier that does not implement that
   profile reports the response as signature-unverified, which is
   neither failed nor absent. response is not part of the composed
   digest (Section 8.2.7).

   An artifact that fails verification is still an artifact member.  The
   verifier reports the failure on that member.  It is not a fourth
   outcome, and the verifier MUST NOT report it as a successful grant.

   An empty-history Bundle (Section 3.1) is an artifact member that
   shows no history.  It is neither a failure nor an absence.  A
   responder whose history was cleared, or never started, can answer
   with one.

   *Each member keeps its own claims.* A verifier MUST verify every
   carried member bundle as an Evidence Bundle under this document.  It
   MUST report that bundle's graph closure, interval coverage and per-
   record membership (Section 6), and its disclosures and
   countersignatures, _per member_.

   The verifier MUST NOT merge one member's claims with another
   member's, or with the containing Bundle's.  The containing Bundle's
   claims are about its own records only.  A refusal or absence member
   is not a failed claim: a verifier reports it as what it is.

8.2.3.  Composition Closure

   A composed/v1 block makes a fourth completeness claim, _composition
   closure_. It holds when every member is either:




Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 22]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   *  present, meaning its body is carried and reproduces its digest; or

   *  declared missing, meaning its id is listed in missing and it
      carries no body.

   The claim has three results:

   *  *pass*: every member is present and missing is absent or empty.

   *  *withheld* (finding declared_incomplete): every member is present
      or declared missing, and at least one is declared missing.

   *  *fail*: in any of these cases:

      -  a member neither carries a body nor is listed in missing;

      -  a listed member carries a body;

      -  a carried body does not reproduce its digest;

      -  missing names an undeclared member.

   A verifier MUST report composition closure separately from the three
   per-member claims and from the containing Bundle's claims.  It MUST
   NOT report it as one of them.

   Declaring a member missing withholds its body, not its existence.
   Its id, observer, request_digest, outcome and digest remain in the
   composed digest.

   Composition closure covers declared members only.  A participant that
   is in neither members nor not_requested is not visible to a verifier
   from this block (Section 12.1).

8.2.4.  Controller Binding Records

   A _controller binding record_ is a JSON object in which the holders
   of two signing keys both sign one statement about how the two keys
   are related.  It is the linkage evidence of the controller_binding
   join basis (Section 8.2.5).  Its signatures show only that whoever
   held each private key signed the record.  They do not show who holds
   either key, and this document never states who a key holder is.

   A binding record is not a member of the composed/v1 block.  It is
   delivered beside the containing Bundle, with any revocation
   statements that withdraw it; how they are delivered is outside this
   document.  The block binds a record only through a join's
   identifier_digest.



Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 23]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   In this section a _key identifier_ is 64 lowercase hexadecimal
   characters; for an Ed25519 [RFC8032] key, the one key type this
   document defines, it is the raw 32-byte public key.  A _time_ is an
   [RFC3339] date-time in UTC with a "Z" suffix and seconds precision.
   A root record's timestamp, against which a binding is checked, can
   also carry fractional seconds.  Times compare as instants.

   controller-binding = {
     binding_version: "controller-binding/0",
     claim: binding-claim,
     method: tstr,                  ; a verification method token
     keys: [key-id, key-id],        ; two distinct keys, ascending
     issued_at: binding-time,       ; the ceremony, as stated
     not_before: binding-time,      ; not later than issued_at
     not_after: binding-time,       ; later than issued_at
     revocation: "revocation_statement/0",
     nonce: tstr,                   ; non-empty, chosen per ceremony
     sigs: [* binding-sig],
     ? key_types: [tstr, tstr],     ; one per entry of keys, in order
     ? delegator: key-id,           ; delegated_by only: one of keys
     ? credential: binding-credential,
                                    ; same_organization_credentialed
   }

   binding-claim = "same_controller_asserted" / "delegated_by" /
                   "same_organization_credentialed"
   binding-sig = { key_id: key-id, sig: any }   ; sig-hex for ed25519
   binding-credential = {
     issuer: key-id,
     method: tstr,
     digest: digest-hex,
   }

   binding-revocation = {
     revocation_version: "controller-binding-revocation/0",
     binding: digest-hex,           ; the binding digest
     key_id: key-id,                ; one of the binding's keys
     revoked_at: binding-time,
     sig: sig-hex,
   }

   key-id       = text .regexp "[0-9a-f]{64}"
   sig-hex      = text .regexp "[0-9a-f]{128}"
   binding-time = text .regexp
     "[0-9]{4}-[0-9]{2}-[0-9]{2}T[0-9]{2}:[0-9]{2}:[0-9]{2}Z"

   The claim types, the member each adds, the method this document
   defines for each, and what the two key holders state:



Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 24]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   *  same_controller_asserted: no added member; dual_signature/0.  One
      controller holds both keys.

   *  delegated_by: adds delegator; dual_signature/0.  The holder of
      delegator delegated to the holder of the other key, who accepted.

   *  same_organization_credentialed: adds credential;
      dual_signature_credential/0.  Both keys are held within one
      organization, as a credential from a third key states.

   A binding record is malformed when it lacks a member, carries a
   member the CDDL does not list, or has a value outside its form; when
   it carries delegator and its claim is not delegated_by, or credential
   and its claim is not same_organization_credentialed, or lacks the
   member its claim adds; when its two keys are equal or not in
   ascending order; when not_before is later than issued_at or not_after
   is not later than it; when nonce is empty; when delegator is not one
   of keys; when both keys are ed25519 keys and a sig is not a sig-hex;
   or when its method is one this document defines for another claim
   type.  A method token this document does not define is well formed.
   A binding record or revocation statement that is not a JSON object is
   malformed. delegated_by states a relation, not a scope: a verifier
   reads no authority from it.

   *Key types.* key_types, when present, names the type of each key in
   keys, in its order.  When it is absent, both keys are ed25519 keys.
   This document defines one key type, ed25519: the key identifier is
   the raw Ed25519 public key, and the key signs with Ed25519.  A record
   whose key_types names any other token is well formed.  Its sig values
   are not held to sig-hex, and a verifier that does not implement that
   key type treats the record as under a method it does not implement.

   *Signing input and binding digest.* The signing input is the UTF-8
   octets of the JCS [RFC8785] serialization of the record without sigs.
   Every other member is covered.  The _binding digest_ is the lowercase
   hexadecimal SHA-256 of the signing input.  It does not cover sigs, so
   a binding keeps its digest if a key signs it again.  Two ceremonies
   over the same keys differ in nonce and so have two digests.

   *Methods.* Under dual_signature/0, sigs has exactly two entries:
   sigs[0].key_id is keys[0], sigs[1].key_id is keys[1], and each sig is
   that key's signature over the signing input; for an ed25519 key, its
   Ed25519 signature in lowercase hexadecimal.  A record with one
   signature, a third entry, or a signature that does not verify is not
   signed by both keys.  One key cannot bind another key to itself,
   because each key signs the other key's identifier.
   dual_signature_credential/0 requires the same two signatures.  Its
   credential names a signed object from the key credential.issuer,



Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 25]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   delivered beside the record, whose SHA-256 is credential.digest and
   which is verified by its native method credential.method.  This
   document registers no credential format.  A credential never decides
   whether a binding applies, and a verifier MUST NOT present the
   organization it names as an identity.

   *Revocation.* Either key holder can withdraw a binding with a
   revocation statement (binding-revocation).  A statement _applies_ to
   a binding when it is well formed, binding is the binding digest,
   key_id is one of the binding's keys, and sig is that key's Ed25519
   signature over the UTF-8 JCS serialization of the statement without
   sig.  A statement names a binding by digest, so it reaches every copy
   of the record.  From revoked_at on, the binding is not in effect.
   revoked_at is the revoking key holder's statement; a backdated
   revocation returns the joins it covered to unjoined, never further.
   A verifier knows only the statements it received.

   *When a binding applies.* A binding record _applies_ to two key
   identifiers at a set of times T when all of these hold:

   1.  it is well formed;

   2.  the verifier implements its method and the type of both keys, and
       both bound keys signed it under that method;

   3.  its keys are exactly the two key identifiers, in either order;

   4.  for every T, not_before <= T < not_after;

   5.  no revocation statement the verifier received that applies to it
       has a revoked_at at or before any T.

   *What a binding never states.* A binding is a statement by both key
   holders that their two keys are not independent issuers.  A verifier
   MUST NOT present a binding record as establishing who controls either
   key; a person, name, account, organization identity or legal
   relationship; that the keys were under one control at any time,
   beyond the window and claim both key holders stated; or that two keys
   with no binding between them have distinct controllers.

8.2.5.  Joins

   A join declares that two members' records are about the same thing,
   and how that is established.  The producer DECLARES a state; a
   verifier DERIVES it again from the carried members.  A declaration
   that does not match the derivation is a failed composed/v1 check
   (finding join_state_mismatch).  It is never a pass.




Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 26]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   The states derive as follows:

   *  *one_sided*: exactly one of the two members has outcome artifact
      with records, and the other has none: it is not an artifact, or it
      is an empty-history Bundle (Section 3.1).  Under
      controller_binding, a join with an empty-history member is
      one_sided only when the two are linked (below).

   *  *unjoined*: neither member has outcome artifact with records; or
      one has records, the other is an empty-history Bundle, and the
      controller_binding linkage does not link them; or both have
      records, but the linkage basis does not establish that their
      records are about the same thing.

   *  *agree*: both members are artifacts with records and are linked,
      and every compare pointer resolves in both root records to values
      with identical JCS serializations.  A join with no compare and a
      link is agree.

   *  *mismatch*: both members are artifacts with records and are
      linked, and at least one compare pointer resolves to different
      values, or does not resolve on one side.

   Linkage is evaluated on each artifact member's root record (its
   bundle's root).  This document defines the derivation for three
   bases:

   *  *pre_agreed_identifier*: pointer selects a string in each root
      record.  The members are linked when SHA-256 of that string's
      UTF-8 octets equals identifier_digest on both sides.  The
      identifier itself never enters the block.

   *  *shared_artifact_digest*: pointer selects a digest string in each
      root record.  The members are linked when the two values are
      identical.

   *  *controller_binding*: pointer selects a key identifier string in
      each root record, for example /key_id, and identifier_digest is a
      binding digest (Section 8.2.4).  The members are linked when a
      delivered binding record whose binding digest equals
      identifier_digest applies to the two selected values, with T the
      timestamp of each root record
      ([I-D.mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule]).  A verifier that received
      no record with that digest, or only well-formed records with that
      digest whose method or key types it does not implement, reports
      the join as not_derivable; so does a join without pointer or
      identifier_digest.  Any other record with that digest that does
      not apply (malformed, not signed by both keys, other keys, outside



Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 27]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


      its window, or revoked) leaves the members unjoined.  A join on
      this basis states that the two root records are under keys whose
      holders bound them, as both of them stated it; it says nothing
      about who those holders are.

      When one member is an empty-history Bundle (Section 3.1), the
      derivation still runs.  That member's key identifier is the key
      that signed its checkpoint.cose, as that signature authenticates
      it, and its T is that checkpoint's timestamp; pointer selects the
      key identifier in the other member's root record only.  Linked
      gives one_sided, never agree, because nothing was compared; not
      linked gives unjoined.  An empty-history member without a
      checkpoint.cose that verifies has no key, and the join is
      not_derivable.

   The bases issued_receipt (the two records cite one Receipt) and
   same_interval (the two records fall in one declared interval) are
   reserved for a later specification that defines their derivation.  A
   block MAY declare joins on them; such joins are digest-covered.  A
   verifier that does not implement a derivation for the basis reports
   such a join as not_derivable.  It MUST NOT report the join as a
   derived agreement, and MUST NOT count it as corroboration.

   A join one of whose artifact members is declared missing is also
   not_derivable.

   On a derived mismatch, a verifier reports each member's value at each
   differing pointer, and names no winner.

8.2.6.  Observers and Redundancy

   observers[] states, for each member, which observer produced it, in
   what role, and in which custody_domain. role is a short token naming
   where the observer sits, for example responder, runtime or
   network_boundary; this document does not define a role vocabulary,
   and a verifier reports the role without interpreting it.  A custody
   domain is an opaque label that the composing party assigns.  Two
   observers are in the same custody domain exactly when their labels
   are identical octet sequences.

   *Same custody is redundant, not corroborating.* For every join that
   derives agree, a verifier MUST report the pair as redundant in any of
   these cases:

   *  both members name the same observer;

   *  the two observers have the same custody_domain;




Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 28]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   *  the two members' root records carry the same key_id;

   *  the join's basis is controller_binding: the holders of the two
      keys have stated that the keys are not independent issuers
      (Section 8.2.4).

   It MUST present the result as "redundant, not corroborating", with
   the reason.  A controller_binding join therefore never yields
   corroboration.  Otherwise it MAY report the pair as corroborating,
   and it MUST qualify that as resting on _declared_ custody.

   Custody labels are the composing party's declaration.  Like producer-
   key/v1 (Section 8.1), they can only downgrade a result.  Identical
   labels make a pair redundant.  Distinct labels never establish that
   two observers are independent, and a verifier MUST NOT present them
   as doing so.

   Corroboration is derived; it is never declared, and it is not part of
   the block.  A verifier lists results per pair.  It MUST NOT combine
   them into a count or score.

8.2.7.  Composed Digest

   The composed digest is the digest a judgment cites as the evidence
   set it was made from.  It is computed only from the declarations in
   the block, so it recomputes from the containing Bundle alone.  It
   stays the same whether or not the member bodies are carried.

   1.  Build the _digest input_ D, a JSON object with exactly these five
       members:

       *  kind: the string "composed/v1".

       *  members: for each member, an object with exactly its id,
          observer, request_digest, outcome and digest.  Sort the
          objects in ascending order of id.

       *  observers: for each observer, an object with exactly its id,
          role and custody_domain.  Sort in ascending order of id.

       *  joins: for each join, an object with its members, basis and
          state, plus whichever of pointer, identifier_digest and
          compare the join carries (absent members stay absent, never
          null).  Sort in ascending order of members[0], then
          members[1], then basis, then pointer (the empty string when
          absent). compare keeps its carried order.  Use [] when the
          block has no joins.




Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 29]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


       *  not_requested: the block's not_requested sorted ascending, or
          [] when it has none.

       Identifiers are ASCII (label-id), so ascending order is octet
       order.  The order in which arrays are carried does not affect the
       digest.

   2.  composed_digest is the lowercase hexadecimal SHA-256 of the UTF-8
       octets of the JCS [RFC8785] serialization of D.  This is the
       canonicalization algorithm jcs of
       [I-D.mih-sokolov-scitt-payload-binding]: RFC 8785 with no
       normalization pass, SHA-256, bare hex.  D contains no numbers.

   3.  Member bodies, each member's response, missing and
       composed_digest itself are excluded.

   A verifier MUST recompute composed_digest and report a mismatch as a
   failed composed/v1 check.  It MUST NOT report the recomputed value as
   the digest of any member, nor as the containing Bundle's digest.

   The containing Bundle's own digest (Section 9) still covers the whole
   block, bodies included.  A countersignature over the containing
   Bundle therefore binds the composition and everything carried with
   it.  The composed digest binds only the evidence set.

   Because the composed digest covers each member's outcome, observer
   and digest, all observers' roles and custody labels, and every join
   declaration, changing any of them changes the composed digest.  A
   judgment that cites a composed digest therefore also binds the
   declared custody, and with it the redundancy result a verifier
   derives from that custody.

8.2.8.  Verifying composed/v1

   A verifier that implements composed/v1 MUST report each of the
   following separately:

   1.  the recomputed composed digest, and whether it matches;

   2.  per member: outcome, body carried or declared missing, digest
       reproduced, and, for a carried member bundle, its own three
       completeness claims, no history for an empty-history Bundle
       (Section 3.1), and, when it carries response, whether the
       response's digests match and its signature state;

   3.  composition closure;





Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 30]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   4.  per join: the declared state, the derived state (or
       not_derivable), and whether they match;

   5.  per derived agree: redundant (with reason) or corroborating on
       declared custody.

   A verifier that does not implement composed/v1 follows Section 8: it
   reports the block as uninterpreted and integrity-covered.  It MUST
   NOT present any member, join or composed digest as checked.

8.3.  Provisional Extension Kinds

   The interim registry (Section 14) also records two extension kinds

9.  Bundle Digest and Countersignatures

   The bundle digest is SHA-256(UTF8(JCS(canonical bundle form))),
   rendered as 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters.  The canonical
   bundle form is the Bundle with countersignatures omitted.
   countersignatures is excluded so that independent parties can add
   signatures without changing the digest they sign; every other present
   Bundle member, including extensions, participates.

   countersignatures, when present, is an array of objects.  Each object
   MUST carry type and the type's signature encoding, and signs the
   bundle digest.  An object whose signer is a party other than the
   producer is a countersignature (Section 2).  This document does not
   define who populates the slot or whether a relying party should rely
   on a given signer.














Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 31]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   The registered types are cose-sign1 and countersign/v1.  A cose-sign1
   object is {type: "cose-sign1", signature: "<base64url tagged
   COSE_Sign1>"}. It is a reserved neutral slot whose population and
   verification are future scope; a current verifier MUST surface each
   such entry as unverified and MUST NOT present pass-through data as a
   verified countersignature.  The countersign/v1 object is defined in
   Section 9.1.  A verifier MUST report an entry of a type it does not
   implement as unverified and MUST NOT let it change any other result.
   An entry with no type member is the legacy form of countersign/v1.  A
   verifier MUST process it as countersign/v1, and its signing input
   then uses the value countersign/v1 for type.  A producer MUST NOT
   emit the legacy form.

9.1.  The countersign/v1 Entry

   {

   [RFC8032] public key as 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters: the key
   itself, not a digest of it, so that a verifier holding only the entry
   can check the signature offline.

   over is the bundle digest (Section 9) of the Bundle the countersigner
   recomputed.

   statement says what the countersigner recomputed and with what
   result.

9.2.  Verifying a countersign/v1 Entry

   A verifier MUST, for each countersign/v1 entry:

   1.  recompute the bundle digest and compare it to over.  An entry
       whose over differs is not a countersignature of this Bundle and
       MUST be reported as invalid;

   2.  verify signature over the signing input under signer.key_id.  An
       entry whose signature fails is invalid, and its checks MUST NOT
       be displayed as results;

   3.  determine independence as in Section 9.3.

   A verifier MAY resolve signer.key_id against a directory of
   countersigners that it or the relying party chooses, to put a name to
   the key.  No directory is privileged, and this document defines none.
   A verifier reports each countersign/v1 entry as exactly one of
   invalid, not independent, unresolved signer (independent, and the key
   is in no directory consulted), or resolved (independent, and the key
   is found).  It MUST attribute each check and result to the signer, as
   the signer's statement, and MUST NOT present them as its own
   findings.

9.3.  Self-Countersignature

   A countersignature is a self-countersignature when signer.key_id
   equals the producer's key: the kid of a Producer Envelope over any
   record in records ([I-D.mih-scitt-agent-action-capsule]), the
   public_key of a well-formed producer-key/v1 extension (Section 8.1),
   or any other key the verifier holds for that producer.  A self-
   countersignature is a well-formed entry that does not meet the
   definition of a countersignature, because its signer is the producer.
   A verifier MUST NOT reject it for being one, and MUST render it as
   not independent, never as a countersignature by a party other than
   the producer.  The verifier computes independence itself for each
   entry; no entry member and no directory entry can change that result,
   and a producer key declaration can only change it to not independent.

10.  Fragment Codec

   The common permalink codec is exact.  To encode a Bundle in a URL
   fragment, a producer MUST serialize the whole Bundle with JCS,
   including recursive sort_keys object-member ordering, UTF-8 encode
   the resulting JSON text, and encode those bytes with RFC 4648
   base64url [RFC4648] Section 5 without padding.  The resulting
   unescaped ASCII string is the fragment value after #. A decoder MUST
   base64url-decode the unpadded fragment, UTF-8 decode it, parse it as
   JSON, and then apply the Bundle checks in this document.

   The /v/<id> route carries a Bundle of one using this same codec;
   /bundle uses no different decoder.  The path is a locator only.  URL
   fragments are not sent in HTTP requests, so a hosted service receives
   a Bundle only when it is otherwise uploaded or embedded in the
   returned report.

11.  Verification

   verification, when present, is producer self-report.  It MAY record
   tool versions, performed checks, or findings, but it is not proof and
   MUST NOT replace independent verification of Capsules, disclosures,
   closure, certificates, checkpoints, or countersignatures.  A verifier
   MUST label it as producer self-report.

   A conforming verifier MUST at minimum compute the Bundle digest,
   verify each enclosed Capsule under the base profile, apply Section 4
   and Section 5, and report graph closure, interval coverage, and per-
   record membership as separate results.  It MUST distinguish WITHHELD,
   REVEALED-match, REVEALED-mismatch, declared incomplete, and absent or
   invalid membership proofs.  It MUST apply Section 6.1 to every
   Bundle, so that a certificate mode it does not implement is reported
   as not established, never as failed.

12.  Security Considerations

   The Bundle does not make omitted evidence disappear.  Its explicit
   missing declaration is necessary to prevent a short report from being
   represented as a complete closure.  Range endpoints are not a
   substitute for per-record membership: endpoint-only proofs leave
   interior substitution and deletion undetected.  Disclosures are not
   signatures and are untrusted until their DE-3 digest comparison
   succeeds.  Fragments can be copied or changed by a party hosting a
   link, so every received Bundle requires independent verification.
   Unknown extension kinds are integrity-covered but semantically
   untrusted.





Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 35]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   A producer-selected certificate proves that each carried record is in
   the log; it says nothing about the records the producer left out, so
   a verifier never presents one as complete (Section 6.2).  Reporting
   an unimplemented certificate mode as not established rather than
   invalid keeps a failure meaningful: a verifier that reported every
   unfamiliar mode as invalid would report an honest Bundle and an
   altered one identically.  The cost is a downgrade: whoever alters a
   Bundle can name an unknown mode, or put a non-record leaf of an
   undefined type where a record or a padding leaf was, and so turn a
   failing per-record membership into one that is not established
   (Section 6.1, Section 6.3).  Neither change can make a claim
   established.  A relying party therefore treats "not established" as
   unverified, never as a pass.  A padding leaf is accepted only with
   its full content, never as a bare digest, because a bare leaf value
   could be the identifier of a Capsule the producer chose not to show;
   recomputing the value from padding content, whose only free member is
   a random nonce, rules that out (Section 6.3).

   A countersignature's statement and signer are what a relying party
   reads, so both are inside the signing input (Section 9.1); a
   signature over the bundle digest alone would let anyone holding the
   Bundle rewrite a failed result as established, or attribute the entry
   to a different signer identifier, without detection.  A verified
   signature establishes who made the statement, not that the relying
   party should rely on that party: signer trust, and the choice of any
   directory used to put a name to a key, are outside this document.  A
   self-countersignature is not a second party's check, and rendering it
   as one would present the producer's own claim as independent
   corroboration; Section 9.3 forbids that.  A producer-key/v1
   declaration can only cause an entry to be reported as not
   independent: a producer that declares a key it does not hold weakens
   only its own Bundle's countersignatures, and a producer that
   countersigns with a key it does not declare is still reported at most
   as an unresolved signer unless a directory the verifier chose lists
   that key.  Results are listed per check and never combined, because
   an aggregate would hide a failed or not checked result behind a
   total.

12.1.  composed/v1

   *  *Omitted participants.* The composing party chooses whom to list.
      Composition closure proves that every _declared_ member is present
      or declared missing.  It cannot reveal a participant that the
      composing party never listed. not_requested makes a known omission
      explicit.  Completeness against the real set of participants needs
      an independent statement of that set, such as a coordinator's
      sealed topology.  A verifier MUST NOT describe composition closure
      as completeness of participation.



Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 36]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   *  *Declarations versus derivations.* Declared join states are re-
      derived; a mismatch fails.  Declared custody can only downgrade
      (Section 8.2.6).  Nothing a producer declares can make two
      observers independent.

   *  *Controller bindings.* A binding can only make an agreement
      redundant (Section 8.2.6); no binding, revocation or failed
      signature can make two members corroborating.  Two keys bound by
      their holders can sign agreeing records, and without the
      redundancy rule they would read as two issuers.  A verifier knows
      only the revocation statements it received: the party presenting a
      composition can leave out a revocation, and a controller_binding
      agreement is then reported redundant all the same, never more.  A
      revocation the producer did not account for derives unjoined and
      fails a declared agree (join_state_mismatch).

   *  *No history.* An empty-history Bundle (Section 3.1) is its
      producer's statement that its stream holds no record.  Its signed
      checkpoint shows that the stream exists, under which key, and the
      size of its log at that time.  It carries no certificate, so
      nothing shows that no leaf of that log is a record.  A verifier
      MUST NOT present no history as evidence that no action took place.
      Because it is an artifact, never an absence, a cleared or
      unstarted history cannot be read as a responder that did not
      answer.

   *  *Outcomes.* Keeping refusal and absence distinct is evidentiary:
      synthesizing either one from the other forges the stronger record
      from the weaker.

12.2.  Category Coverage

   A category declaration is the producer's statement about itself
   (Section 7).  A producer that chooses its own categories can declare
   whatever is favorable to it; this is why a private set is never
   reported as registered, and why a verifier reports properties rather
   than a verdict of completeness.  A signed ABSENT is attributable and
   can be contradicted by records a counterparty holds, but it is not
   evidence that nothing exists.  Including the Bundle's root in the
   signing input stops a declaration made for one root record from being
   presented with another; a countersignature over the bundle digest is
   what binds it to the whole Bundle.

13.  Privacy Considerations

   A Bundle reveals a preimage only through its disclosure overlay
   (Section 5); a member absent from the overlay stays WITHHELD.




Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 37]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


   A range that carries padding leaves (Section 6.3) shows where padding
   runs begin and end, and padding is appended before a checkpoint is
   cut.  Such a range therefore reveals some of the log's earlier
   checkpoint sizes, which padding exists to blur (Section 12.1 of
   [I-D.mih-agent-evidence-layer]).  A producer that does not want to
   show them uses a producer-selected certificate (Section 6.2), which
   carries no positions other than those of the records it carries.

   A cleared history's empty-history Bundle (Section 3.1) carries a
   checkpoint signed by the same key as the cleared stream.  Clearing a
   history therefore does not unlink that key from it.

   For the composed/v1 extension (Section 8.2):

   *  Join identifiers enter only as digests (identifier_digest).

   *  A delivered controller binding record (Section 8.2.4) reveals that
      the holders of its two keys bound them, its claim type and its
      window.  It carries no name, and it is delivered only to whom the
      presenting party chooses.

   *  custody_domain is an opaque label and SHOULD NOT be a network
      address, an account identifier or a personal name.

   *  Withholding a member's body (missing) keeps the composed digest
      stable.  A judgment can therefore be checked against a composition
      presented to an audience that may not see every member.

14.  IANA Considerations

   IANA is requested to create the "AAC Evidence Bundle Parameters"
   registry group with these Specification Required registries, using
   [RFC8174] and [RFC2119] terminology and the designated-expert
   criteria of [RFC8126]:

   1.  "Evidence Bundle kind", initial value evidence-bundle/v2.

   2.  "Evidence Bundle extension kind", initial values producer-key/v1
       (Section 8.1) and composed/v1 (Section 8.2).  A registered
       specification defines each block; private x- prefixed kinds are
       not registered.  The kinds sd-jwt-issuers/v1 and disclosure-
       policy-decisions/v1 (Section 8.3) are not initial values.

   3.  "Evidence Bundle countersignature type", initial values cose-
       sign1 (Section 9) and countersign/v1 (Section 9.1).

Change Log

   Since -01:

   *  Certificate modes (Section 6.1): completeness.selection names the
      mode of completeness_certificate; a verifier reports a mode it
      does not implement as not established
      (certificate_mode_unsupported), never as failed or invalid.

   *  Defined the producer-selected certificate (Section 6.2), in the
      shape producers already emit: selection producer-selected, no
      range, each record proven on its own; interval coverage is never
      established for it.  Added none to payloads_mode.

   *  Non-record leaves (Section 6.3): a range covers padding leaves in
      completeness_certificate.non_record_leaves, each carried in full;
      stated the padding leaf's serialization and identifier, the per-



Mih & Zhang                Expires 4 May 2027                  [Page 41]

Internet-Draft             AAC Evidence Bundle              October 2026


      position accounting rule for per-record membership, and that a
      leaf of an undefined type leaves the claim not established, never
      established.  Stated that per-record membership is the claim, and
      that unknown completeness_certificate members are ignored.
      Privacy Considerations: what a padded range reveals.  Security
      Considerations: reporting an unimplemented mode or an undefined
      leaf type as not established can turn a failure into not
      established, never into established; why a padding leaf is carried
      in full.

   *  Category coverage (Section 7): the OPTIONAL category_coverage
      member, a producer's signed declaration of one status per category
      of a named category set (AVAILABLE, PARTIAL, WITHHELD, DELETED,
      ABSENT, UNDISCLOSED; UNDISCLOSED requires policy_uri), answering
      the Evidence Request categories field; five verifier properties,
      reported separately and never combined; it declares and does not
      prove.  Created the "Evidence Bundle category set" registry, with
      no initial values.  The Abstract mentions the declaration.

   *  composed/v1: defined the controller binding record
      (Section 8.2.4): its members and claim types, its signing input
      and binding digest, the methods dual_signature/0 and
      dual_signature_credential/0, revocation statements, and when a
      binding applies; and the controller_binding join basis with its
      derivation (Section 8.2.5).  A controller_binding agreement is
      always redundant, never corroborating (Section 8.2.6).  A verifier
      that implements only -01 reads a block that carries a
      controller_binding join as malformed, since -01's join-basis does
      not list it.  Security and Privacy Considerations: controller
      bindings.  A binding record MAY carry key_types; this document
      defines the ed25519 key type, and a record under any other is
      treated as under a method a verifier does not implement.

   *  Empty-history Bundles (Section 3.1): a Bundle with no records and
      no root shows that a stream has no history to disclose, whether it
      was never started or its history was cleared (a new stream, with a
      new log_id).  It is well formed and reported as no history, never
      as failed and never as an absence.  In composed/v1 it is an
      artifact member, and a join with it derives one_sided; a
      controller_binding join derives from the key that signed its
      checkpoint, and is not_derivable without one.  Security
      Considerations: no history.  Privacy Considerations: a cleared
      history's checkpoint is signed by the cleared stream's key.

   *  Cites Evidence Request -01 and, informatively, Evidence Layer -00
      and the Canonicalization Declaration -06.
